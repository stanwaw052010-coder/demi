In March 2024 we bought twelve cakes in Menghai that were not what the seller said they were. We had sold eight of them before we worked it out. This is what happened and what we did about it.

## The purchase

We were after an affordable single-garden sheng from the autumn of 2021, something between twenty and thirty euro per hundred grams. A trader we had dealt with twice before offered a batch from a village on the south slope of Nannuo. Twenty-five cakes, of which we took twelve.

The sample we drank at his table was good. Clear Nannuo character, somewhat smoky, with the bitter opening that turns over quickly. We drank three infusions on the spot, paid, and unpacked the cakes in Ghent a week later.

## What did not add up

The first cake we broke open at home was different. Not bad, but broader and sweeter, with less of that specific bitterness and more floral character. The second had a striking amount of stem in it.

So we set them side by side: our cake, our sample packet from the trader, and a Nannuo whose origin we were certain of. The sample matched the reference. Our cakes did not.

The most likely explanation is the simplest, and it is a known practice: the sample came from a different batch than the delivery. Not necessarily in bad faith — material gets blended and batches get combined in the trade, and what exactly went in is often no longer traceable for the middleman either.

What was in our cakes was, with reasonable confidence, autumn leaf from Menghai of mixed origin. It is decent tea. It is simply not what we paid for, and not what our label said.

## What we did

We pulled the remaining four cakes from sale and emailed the eight buyers. Five of them had already drunk part of the tea and liked it. Three wanted their money back, and got it, shipping included, without having to send anything back.

We put it to the trader. His answer was that the batch was correct and that we were mistaken. That is an answer we cannot refute and he cannot prove. We do not buy there any more.

The four remaining cakes are still in our cupboard. We drink them ourselves.

## What we changed

Since then we have one rule we did not have before: the sample must come from the batch we take with us, and the cake the sample was broken from comes along. On larger purchases we break open a random cake from the stack, not the one lying on the table.

For tea we do not collect ourselves, we buy no more than ten percent of a batch without first having had a sample from the actual shipment. That makes us slower and it sometimes costs us a good batch to someone who decides faster.

We also stopped filling in fields we are not sure of. Since then, a garden on the site is either a name or nothing. We no longer write "Yunnan" where we mean "we do not know".

## Why this is here

Because we put a passport on every tea, and a passport is only worth something if it is allowed to be empty now and then. A shop that manages to name a garden for twenty-eight teas out of twenty-eight either knows more than anyone in this trade, or is filling in fields.

And because we would rather you read this from us than discover it yourself in three years.
